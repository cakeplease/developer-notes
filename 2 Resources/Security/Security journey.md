

**Awareness: Vocabulary**

1. A threat is a possible danger that might breach security, and risk is the possibility of a threat occurring.
2. An attacker is an adversary with the mission of gaining access to a product or system.
3. A vulnerability is a weakness in a system, application, or network that is subject to exploitation or misuse.
4. An attack is an attempt to gain unauthorized access.
5. Exfiltration is the unauthorized transfer of information from an information system.

  

**Awareness: Myths and Misconceptions**

- Everyone is attractive to an attacker.
- Security is everyone’s problem.
- An ex and a thief both have the motivation to enter our building.
- Cyber vigilance is easier than you think.
- Security educated and aware people are a strength.
- All devices are equally vulnerable.

  
**Awareness: Attackers**

1. The threat landscape is all the potential threats that exist on the Internet, including the daily addition of new attackers, attacks, and connected Internet devices.
2. The four primary cyber adversaries are cybercriminals, nation-states, hacktivists, and insiders.
3. Cyber-criminals steal, sell personal info, and extort; nation states break into other governments and companies; hacktivists attempt to disrupt the world; insiders become disgruntled and attack from the inside.

  

**Awareness: Data Breach**

1. A data breach is an event in which confidential data has potentially been viewed, stolen or used by an individual unauthorized to do so.
2. The steps of a data breach are research, attack, and exfiltrate.
3. The impact of a data breach includes both our financial and reputational basis.

  

**Awareness: Data Security**

**CIA**

Data Security foundation; confidentiality, integrity, availability.

1. Data security is protecting digital data from unauthorized users, attack, and data breach.
2. Protect customer data by controlling output, never sending over unapproved channels, watch out for tailgating and phishing, and do not leave assets outside your sphere of control.
3. Data security is everyone’s responsibility!

  

**Awareness: Human Factors**

1. he human factors of cyber and physical security include the nature of humans, desire to help, want to please, trusting, and emotional.
2. You are part of the security problem and solution!
3. Continue learning and becoming more aware with security training.

  

**Awareness: Passwords**

1. The purpose of a password is to prove to an IT system that you are whom you say you are.
2. Password-based threats include using the same password on multiple sites, using a weak password, and writing your password on a sticky note.
3. A strong password is one that is long, strong, and unique.
4. A passphrase is a mechanism of creating a password that is easy to remember.
5. Two-step verification protects you by adding a layer of security to your accounts.

  

**Awareness: Phishing 101**

1. Everyone must be vigilant towards phishing.
2. Attackers send phishing e-mails to steal your credentials and install malware on your computer.
3. Attackers use stories that are too good to be true, threats, and attempts at sympathy.
4. When considering an e-mail, remember to identify, decide, and report or delete.

  

  

**Awareness: Privacy**

1. Security is about protecting information; privacy is about controlling who is allowed to access information.
2. PII is information that identifies a specific individual and includes name, address, phone number, social security #, credit cards, and more.
3. Personal and customer privacy is everyone’s responsibility!

  

**Awareness: Social Engineering**

1. Social engineering consists of phishing, baiting, tailgating, pretexting, and dumpster diving.
2. A simple process used by social engineers includes information gathering, relationship building, exploitation, and execution.
3. Remember to trust but verify.

  

**Awareness: Mobile Security**

1. The threats impacting mobile devices include lost devices and installation of malware.
2. Corporate assets should contain only corporate information.
3. To secure your mobile device, remember to regularly update, use trusted apps, protect your phone with a passphrase, and choose your WIFI connections carefully.
